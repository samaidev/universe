## Chapter 1 Introduction: Position and Overview

This chapter sets out the ontological commitment, the basic architecture, and the methodological position of the book. The theory begins from the single question "what is existence?" and immediately converts it into a mathematical setting that can be stated with rigor. Before any concrete structure is developed, we make clear what this theory currently is, what it is not, and how it is to be tested.

### 1.1 Ontology: Information as Existence Itself

The ontological commitment of this book can be stated in a single sentence: the ontology of the universe is infinite, continuous information, namely states in the complex infinite-dimensional Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$. "Information" here is not a metaphor, nor a shorthand adopted for convenience of exposition; it is an ontological commitment: existence itself is a state. In other words, we do not say that the universe "can be described in terms of information"; we say that what the universe is, is precisely such a state. Every statement about the universe is ultimately a statement about $\mathcal{H}$ and the structure defined on it. This commitment is kept literally throughout the book: whenever we ask what the world contains, the answer is "a state and its structure," with no appeal to any more fundamental carrier.

The phrase "infinite and continuous" calls for a brief comment. $\mathcal{H}$ is an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space over the complex field $\mathbb{C}$; the state space itself is not discretized in advance, and if discreteness appears anywhere, it must be generated by the internal structure of the theory rather than presupposed at the ontological level. This order matters: first come the state and its structure; only then do the familiar notions—time, space, entropy—come in. The latter must grow out of the structure of the former, not be installed in advance.

Accordingly, $\mathcal{H}$ in this book does not denote the state space of some particular physical system; it is the space in which the ontology resides. Of $\mathcal{H}$ itself the book retains only the metric structure supplied by the inner product and the norm, and appends no notion of time, space, or particles at the ontological level. Whether these notions can be defined, and how, is precisely the task of the chapters that follow. As for "continuous," it serves as a reminder that this book does not introduce lattice sites or sampling schemes at the ontological level in advance; any discreteness appearing later derives from the iteration structure itself, not from the state space.

### 1.2 The Three Basic Elements

The basic elements of the theory are three, not one postulate. This is the architectural claim of the book, and it must be stated clearly here.

The first element is the self-referential structure: $\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$, that is, the space is isomorphic to the tensor product of an $n$-dimensional complex space with itself; its operator formulation is $n$ isometric embeddings $S_i$ satisfying $\sum_iS_iS_i^*=1$ (the Cuntz relations), and these isometric embeddings generate the Cuntz algebra $O_n$. The second element is the origin state: $\psi_0=\sum_ic_iS_i\psi_0$, $\|\psi_0\|=1$, a normalized fixed point. The third element is the iteration rule $V$: an operator prescription acting on states, adding one node per iteration. It must be emphasized in particular that $V$ is not yet fixed by the first two elements and must be given as an independent element; the flexibility of the theory is concentrated here.

The three elements are mutually independent; each can be stated separately, replaced separately, and tested separately. This is not a preference of presentation but a methodological design: if the theory were packaged as a single postulate, a failure at any one point would implicate the whole, and the failure could not be localized. Under the three-element architecture, whichever element fails, the correction or replacement falls on that element alone, and the rest need not be torn down with it. The book develops the elements one by one: Chapter 2 treats Element One, Chapter 3 treats Element Two, and Chapter 4 treats Element Three. "Replace or test" has a concrete meaning here: if one element is replaced or called into question, whether the remaining parts are still self-consistent can be checked item by item within the theory; this is also why the open questions can be discussed one by one in later chapters.

### 1.3 Methodological Position

The present status of the theory must be stated as it is. It is: a research programme that is internally consistent in its essentials and structurally clear, accompanied by a reproducible minimal model. That is the whole of its present identity. "Structurally clear" has a concrete meaning here: every definition is carried by a definite mathematical object, and every inference can be traced back to one of the three elements; the book does not use statements that have no such bearer.

More explicitly: the theory has not yet produced any prediction that can be tested by experiment; before it yields a definite number or structure, it should not be presented as a correct physical theory. This positional statement sets the tone of the whole book; Chapter 11 will restate it verbatim as the conclusion, and this chapter gives it in advance. Any claim going beyond this formulation—for instance, asserting that the theory is "correct," "verified," or "consistent with experiment"—is not the position of this book.

This determines the standard of testing adopted at the present stage, which is internal: whether the definitions are clear, whether the reasoning is checkable, and whether the minimal model is reproducible. These are strict standards, but they cannot substitute for experimental test; this distinction runs through the entire book.

## Chapter 2 Element One: The Self-Referential Structure and the Cuntz Algebra

This chapter states the first basic element: the self-referential structure. We first give its spatial formulation and its operator formulation, then explain the precise meaning of "self-reference" in this setting, and finally restrict the algebraic facts the book is allowed to use, explicitly, to two, giving a complete derivation of the less obvious one. This chapter is the operator foundation of all subsequent chapters, and its boundary of applicability must therefore be drawn with particular clarity.

### 2.1 Statement of the Self-Referential Structure

The spatial formulation of Element One is a single isomorphism,
$$\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H},$$
where $n$ is a fixed positive integer. It asserts that the whole space is isomorphic to "the tensor product of an $n$-dimensional complex space with the space itself." Each $e_i\otimes\mathcal{H}$ on the right-hand side ($e_i$ a standard basis vector of $\mathbb{C}^n$) is a complete copy of $\mathcal{H}$; the isomorphism says that the whole and the juxtaposition of these $n$ complete copies are one and the same thing.

The operator formulation of Element One is: there exist $n$ isometric embeddings $S_i:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}$ satisfying
$$\sum_iS_iS_i^*=1,$$
called the Cuntz relations. An isometric embedding is a norm-preserving injective linear operator: each $S_i$ maps $\mathcal{H}$, norm-preservingly, into a subspace of itself, namely its range. The Cuntz relations say that the sum of the projections onto the $n$ ranges is exactly the identity operator—the $n$ pieces tile the whole, leaving no gaps; whether these pieces are pairwise disjoint is settled by the derivation in Sec. 2.4.

The spatial and the operator formulations are two faces of one structure: the former says "the whole equals the juxtaposition of $n$ copies," the latter says "the $n$ range subspaces tile the whole." The role of $n$ is the branching number: how many complete copies of itself the whole contains.

Every symbol in this setting has a definite role. $\mathbb{C}^n$ supplies only the $n$ basis vectors $e_i$, which serve as "branch labels," and carries no structure beyond that; all complexity resides on the $\mathcal{H}$ side. The isomorphism thus reads: split the whole into its $n$ branches, and each branch still contains the whole in its entirety; the operator formulation reads: the images of $n$ norm-preserving embeddings fill the entire space. The isomorphism and the Cuntz relations are used interchangeably throughout the book: whenever a decomposition into $\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$ is encountered, $U$ is used to return to $\mathcal{H}$; whenever the branches must be discussed inside $\mathcal{H}$, the $S_i$ are used.

### 2.2 The Cuntz Algebra and the Unitary Isomorphism

The algebra generated by the above $n$ isometric embeddings, together with their adjoints, is called the Cuntz algebra and denoted $O_n$. The name records "the algebra given by the $n$ isometric generators and their Cuntz relations"; "generate" is taken in its literal sense here: $O_n$ is precisely the algebra given by these generators and their Cuntz relations, and this chapter offers no further description of it. As to what further algebraic properties $O_n$ may have, the book speaks only within the two facts listed in Sec. 2.4 and claims nothing beyond them.

From the $S_i$ and the Cuntz relations one obtains directly a unitary isomorphism
$$U:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H},\qquad U\varphi=\sum_ie_i\otimes S_i^*\varphi,$$
where $\varphi$ is an arbitrary state in $\mathcal{H}$ and $e_i$ is the standard basis of $\mathbb{C}^n$. $U$ decomposes any state into its $n$ components $S_i^*\varphi$ and places them respectively into the $n$ copy slots; since $U$ is unitary, this decomposition loses no information.

The status of $U$ deserves emphasis: it turns "spatial self-reference" from an abstract isomorphism in one line into a map that can be computed point by point. Several key corollaries of the later chapters—for example, the concrete form taken by the origin state under the $\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$ decomposition—are built directly on $U$.

$U$ and the $S_i$ may be viewed as the two directions of one structure: $S_i$ sends the whole space, norm-preservingly, into one of the copies—the "packing in" direction—while $U$ splits the whole space into the juxtaposition of $n$ copies—the "unpacking" direction. Both directions are supported by the Cuntz relations, and no new postulate is introduced.

### 2.3 The Meaning of Self-Reference

"Self-reference" has a precise meaning here: the whole is isomorphic to the tensor product of itself with its $n$ copies. The whole $\mathcal{H}$ contains $n$ complete copies of itself, each norm-preserving and uncompressed; the Cuntz relation $\sum_iS_iS_i^*=1$ writes this fact as a one-line operator equation—the sum of the $n$ range projections is the identity. Summarized in one sentence: the sum of the parts equals the whole.

This statement is easily read as literary rhetoric and must be held down at once: it is a precise mathematical statement in which every word has a definition. "Parts" refers to the range subspaces $S_i\mathcal{H}$, which are pairwise disjoint (see the derivation in Sec. 2.4); "sum" refers to the operator sum of the projections; "equals the whole" means that this sum is the identity operator. On the infinite-dimensional $\mathcal{H}$ this structure can be stated rigorously, and the derivations of this chapter involve no approximation.

The boundary must also be pointed out: the self-referential structure is only a spatial setting. By itself it confers no dynamics and promises no evolution; the ingredient of "growth" is brought in by the third element (the iteration rule). Keeping the structural level separate from the evolutionary level is a key step in understanding the architecture of the book.

It should also be made clear that "self-reference" here is a statement at the structural level, referring to the isomorphism between the space and its own copies; it involves no self-reference in the linguistic or semantic sense and manufactures no paradox. Paradoxical difficulties typically arise where a statement refers to itself, whereas the self-reference here is only a rigorous configuration on an infinite-dimensional space. How the copies are mutually coherent is a topic for Element Two and Element Three.

### 2.4 Algebraic Remarks: Two Standard Facts

We now draw the boundary explicitly. Throughout the book, only the following two standard algebraic facts about $O_n$ are used: (a) each $S_i$ is an isometry, $S_i^*S_i=1$, which is the direct expression of isometry; (b) the orthogonality of the ranges, $S_i^*S_j=\delta_{ij}$, follows from the isometries and the Cuntz relations. Beyond these two facts the book claims no further algebraic property of $O_n$: it neither asserts that $O_n$ is "simple" nor uses "nuclear" or any other classificatory description; all subsequent arguments rely only on (a), (b), and the Cuntz relations themselves.

Fact (b) admits a complete and short derivation, the tool being the range projections $p_i=S_iS_i^*$. First, by (a),
$$p_i^2=S_i\big(S_i^*S_i\big)S_i^*=S_iS_i^*=p_i,\qquad p_i^*=p_i,$$
so each $p_i$ is an orthogonal projection, and $\sum_ip_i=\sum_iS_iS_i^*=1$. Next, for any $\xi\in\mathcal{H}$,
$$\|\xi\|^2=\Big\langle\xi,\Big(\sum_kp_k\Big)\xi\Big\rangle=\sum_k\|p_k\xi\|^2.$$
Taking $\xi=p_j\eta$ with $\eta$ arbitrary, the $k=j$ term on the right-hand side is already equal to $\|p_j\eta\|^2$, and since every term is non-negative, all the remaining terms must vanish: $p_ip_j=0$ ($i\neq j$). This is the pairwise orthogonality of the range projections.

From here the orthogonality of $S_i^*S_j$ is read off. Since $p_jS_j=S_jS_j^*S_j=S_j$, we have $S_j=p_jS_j$, and hence for $i\neq j$, $p_jS_i=p_jp_iS_i=0$. Let $T=S_i^*S_j$; then
$$TT^*=S_i^*S_jS_j^*S_i=S_i^*p_jS_i=0,$$
so that for any $\xi$, $\|T^*\xi\|^2=\langle\xi,TT^*\xi\rangle=0$, whence $T=0$, i.e., $S_i^*S_j=0$ ($i\neq j$). Combined with (a), this gives $S_i^*S_j=\delta_{ij}$. The derivation is complete: throughout, only the isometries and the Cuntz relations are used, and no other structural theorem is invoked. Incidentally, the operators $p_i=S_iS_i^*$ appearing in the derivation are precisely the orthogonal projections onto the ranges; this fact likewise uses only (a) and the Cuntz relations and constitutes no additional assertion about $O_n$.

### Key points of this chapter

- Element One: $\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$; its operator formulation is $n$ isometric embeddings $S_i$ satisfying the Cuntz relations $\sum_iS_iS_i^*=1$.
- The $S_i$ generate the Cuntz algebra $O_n$; from this one obtains the unitary isomorphism $U\varphi=\sum_ie_i\otimes S_i^*\varphi$, with $e_i$ the standard basis.
- Meaning of self-reference: the whole is isomorphic to the tensor product of itself with its $n$ copies; the sum of the parts (the range subspaces) equals the whole.
- The book uses only two algebraic facts: the isometries $S_i^*S_i=1$ and the orthogonality of the ranges $S_i^*S_j=\delta_{ij}$; the latter has been derived in full from the orthogonality of the projections $p_i=S_iS_i^*$. Beyond these two, no other algebraic property of $O_n$ is claimed.

## Chapter 3 Element Two: The Origin State and Self-Coherence

This chapter states the second basic element: the origin state. It is a distinguished state fixed within the structure of Element One, satisfying a normalized fixed-point condition. We first give the equation and explain how it is read; we then prove that it admits an entirely equivalent characterization—a derivation requiring only the two facts of Chapter 2; we next explain the precise meaning of "self-coherence"; and finally we explain why this stipulation is not cloning and does not conflict with the no-cloning theorem.

### 3.1 The Normalized Fixed Point

Element Two fixes a state $\psi_0\in\mathcal{H}$ and requires that it satisfy
$$\psi_0=\sum_ic_iS_i\psi_0,\qquad\|\psi_0\|=1,$$
where $c_i\in\mathbb{C}$ are complex coefficients and the $S_i$ are the isometric embeddings given by Element One. Such a $\psi_0$ is called the origin state, and this set of conditions is called the normalized fixed-point condition.

The equation admits a direct reading. Embedding $\psi_0$ successively through each of the $S_i$ yields the $n$ images $S_i\psi_0$; taking the linear superposition of these $n$ images with the complex coefficients $c_i$ yields, exactly, $\psi_0$ itself. This is what "fixed point" means: the state returns to itself under the operation "embed, then superpose." The condition $\|\psi_0\|=1$ is the usual normalization of a state, and nothing further is added.

The relation between Element Two and Element One must be made explicit. The equation uses the $S_i$ of Element One, but the content of Element Two—which state is fixed, and which set of coefficients is chosen—is not given by Element One; the concrete values of the coefficients $c_i$ are an open question in this book (see Chapter 10). This concretely instantiates the architecture in which the three elements can be stated separately and tested separately.

The level at which the equation sits should also be indicated. The $S_i\psi_0$ on the right-hand side use the embeddings of Element One, and the summation uses the linear structure of $\mathcal{H}$; beyond these, the equation requires no other object. The origin state is therefore not an appendage inserted into the system from outside, but a self-consistent determination made inside the structure given by Element One: given the $S_i$ and a set of coefficients, the equation says entirely from within the structure what $\psi_0$ is.

### 3.2 Equivalent Characterization and Two-Way Derivation

The fixed-point condition admits an entirely equivalent characterization:
$$S_j^*\psi_0=c_j\psi_0\quad(j=1,\dots,n),\qquad\sum_j|c_j|^2=1.$$
In the usual language of linear algebra: $\psi_0$ is a common eigenvector of each $S_j^*$, with eigenvalue $c_j$, and the squared moduli of these eigenvalues sum to 1. The equivalence of the two characterizations can be derived in both directions, using only the two facts of Chapter 2. For brevity of exposition, the first form will be called the fixed-point form and the second the eigenform.

From the fixed point to the eigen-characterization: left-multiplying $\psi_0=\sum_ic_iS_i\psi_0$ by $S_j^*$ and using $S_j^*S_i=\delta_{ij}$ gives
$$S_j^*\psi_0=\sum_ic_iS_j^*S_i\psi_0=\sum_ic_i\delta_{ij}\psi_0=c_j\psi_0.$$

From the eigen-characterization back to the fixed point: if $S_j^*\psi_0=c_j\psi_0$, then by the Cuntz relation $\sum_iS_iS_i^*=1$,
$$\sum_ic_iS_i\psi_0=\sum_iS_i\big(S_i^*\psi_0\big)=\sum_iS_iS_i^*\psi_0=\psi_0.$$

The normalization condition can likewise be read off from the same two facts: taking the squared modulus of $S_j^*\psi_0=c_j\psi_0$ and summing over $j$ gives
$$\sum_j|c_j|^2=\sum_j\big\|S_j^*\psi_0\big\|^2=\Big\langle\psi_0,\Big(\sum_jS_jS_j^*\Big)\psi_0\Big\rangle=\|\psi_0\|^2=1.$$
The two forms are therefore fully equivalent, and what follows uses whichever is convenient. It may also be noted that $\sum_j|c_j|^2=1$ and $\|\psi_0\|=1$ are matched to each other: the former is precisely the expression of the latter within the equivalent characterization.

### 3.3 The Meaning of Self-Coherence

The precise meaning of "self-coherence" is: the state remains unchanged under coherent superposition with the images of its own embedding. The term can be decomposed into three components. "Self": each component $S_i\psi_0$ of the superposition is generated from $\psi_0$ itself through a fixed embedding, involving no other state. "Coherent": the superposition coefficients $c_i$ are complex numbers, so the phases enter the superposition directly; this is a linear combination in a vector space, in which definite phase relations are maintained among the components, not a probabilistic mixture. "Unchanged": closure—the result of the superposition returns exactly to $\psi_0$; it is an identity, not an approximation, and not an average.

This closure is the source of the word "origin." The origin state is self-contained within the structure: it is defined by its own images and equals the superposition of its own images; the iterative process (Chapter 4) will set out from this self-contained state. And precisely because it is self-contained, statements about it can be tested entirely within Elements One and Two, without recourse to external assumptions.

A word more is due on "coherent." The difference between coherent superposition and probabilistic mixture is this: in the former the coefficients are complex numbers, the phases participate in the superposition, and the result is still a vector; in the latter, probabilities are assigned to possibilities, and the result is no longer a single vector. The right-hand side of the equation is the former. Since the result of the superposition is exactly $\psi_0$, the phase relations among the images are strictly fixed by the equation, with no arbitrariness. One point of potential confusion should also be clarified: closure does not mean that $\psi_0$ is some "unchanging constant." The components $S_i\psi_0$ may well differ from one another; what the equation requires is that, after composition with the coefficients, they return to $\psi_0$. What is fixed is not the entire system but the identity of this particular state under this particular superposition.

### 3.4 The Non-Cloning Argument

A natural question arises: if a state equals the superposition of $n$ images of itself, does this count as "self-replication," and does it conflict with the no-cloning theorem? The answer of this book is: this is not cloning, and therefore it does not violate the no-cloning theorem. The argument is strictly limited to the following three points.

First, superposition is not copying. $\sum_ic_iS_i\psi_0$ is a linear superposition of the $n$ images of one and the same state, and its result is a vector; copying means producing several identical copies independent of one another. A linear superposition produces no copies; the two are different by definition. Second, the theory defines no cloning operation for arbitrary states. Nowhere in the framework is there a map that takes an arbitrary input state and outputs a copy of it. The $S_i$ are fixed isometric embeddings; they act on all states, but they are constrained by the Cuntz relations and are not operators whose purpose or effect is replication, and the framework contains no other mechanism claimed to copy states.

Third, the fixed-point condition is a closed superposition constraint on a single particular state. The equation concerns only $\psi_0$ and a set of coefficients $c_i$: it constrains how this one particular state is composed from its own images, not a universal transformation rule valid for arbitrary states. The constraint is closed: it involves no external states and requires no operation that turns $\psi_0$ into any other state.

Putting the three points together, one sees that "self-coherence" and "cloning" belong to two different conceptual categories: the former is a closed condition, internal to the structure, on a single particular state; the latter is a replication operation on arbitrary states. All the arguments of this book require only the former; the latter is never defined or used. The situation targeted by the no-cloning theorem therefore never arises, and "violation" does not come into question. Beyond the three points above, the book introduces no further quantum-information theorems.

### Key points of this chapter

- Element Two: the origin state satisfies the normalized fixed point $\psi_0=\sum_ic_iS_i\psi_0$, $\|\psi_0\|=1$.
- Equivalent characterization: $S_j^*\psi_0=c_j\psi_0$ with $\sum_j|c_j|^2=1$; the two-way derivation uses only the isometries and the Cuntz relations.
- Meaning of self-coherence: the state remains unchanged under coherent superposition with the images of its own embedding; the closure is an exact identity, not an approximation.
- Non-cloning argument: superposition is not copying; the theory defines no cloning operation for arbitrary states; the fixed-point condition is a closed superposition constraint on a single particular state. Hence this is not cloning and does not violate the no-cloning theorem.
- The concrete values of the coefficients $c_i$ are an open question (Chapter 10).

## Chapter 4 Element Three: The Iteration Rule and Conservation of Information

The previous two chapters presented the first two elements of the theory: the self-referential structure and the origin state. But spatial structure and initial conditions do not by themselves yield motion—with nothing beyond $\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$ and the fixed point $\psi_0$, the world could still be static. This chapter introduces the third element: the iteration rule $V$. We first explain why it must be given as an independent element, and then state its form and its global properties precisely.

### 4.1 The Independence of V

Element One supplies the spatial structure: the self-reference $\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$, together with the family of isometric embeddings $S_i$ that satisfy the Cuntz relations and generate the Cuntz algebra $\mathcal{O}_n$. Element Two supplies a distinguished state: the normalized fixed point $\psi_0$. Taken together, the two still describe a static skeleton. From them one cannot derive a rule that "advances from stage to stage": more than one mode of iteration is compatible with the same family $S_i$ and the same $\psi_0$, and the first two elements do not suffice to distinguish among these modes. The iteration rule $V$ must therefore be given explicitly as a third, independent element, not treated as a conclusion derived from Elements One and Two. This is part of the book's architectural claim: the three elements are mutually independent and can be stated separately, and can therefore be replaced or examined separately. Listing $V$ as an element in its own right is precisely what allows this link of the theory to be inspected and exchanged on its own.

This arrangement also answers where the theory's flexibility resides. Element One and Element Two are quite rigid: the Cuntz relations and the fixed-point condition leave almost no freedom. The choice of $V$, by contrast, leaves genuine room—which operator family $A_i$ to take, and what additional structure to require of it, is not yet fixed (the values of the $A_i$ and the structure required of them are listed among the open questions in Chapter 10). The flexibility of the theory is concentrated here: change $V$, and the shape of the entire subsequent chain changes with it. The character of the test to which this freedom is subject should also be stated in advance: for one concrete choice of $V$, Chapter 8 gives a negative (falsifying) test, which rules out a certain class of $V$ but does not constitute a validation of the theory.

### 4.2 The Form of V

The iteration rule is taken to be an isometric embedding $V:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$ of the form

$$V\psi=\sum_i e_i\otimes A_i\psi,$$

where the $e_i$ are the standard basis of $\mathbb{C}^n$ and the $A_i$ are a given family of operators. It has the same shape as the unitary isomorphism $U\varphi=\sum_i e_i\otimes S_i^*\varphi$ of Element One, but different content. First, the $A_i$ are not determined by the Cuntz operators; they are a newly introduced family of operators. Second, the $A_i$ are required only to satisfy $\sum_iA_i^*A_i=1$ and non-commutativity; no further requirement as complete as the Cuntz relations is imposed. In pictures: $V$ writes every state of $\mathcal{H}$ simultaneously into $n$ branches, the $i$-th branch receiving $A_i\psi$. The number of branches $n$ is fixed by Element One; what is written into each branch is decided by the $A_i$.

The condition $\sum_iA_i^*A_i=1$ is precisely what suffices for $V$ to be an isometry, and the verification takes one line. For any $\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{H}$:

$$\langle V\varphi,V\psi\rangle=\sum_{i,j}\langle e_i\otimes A_i\varphi,\;e_j\otimes A_j\psi\rangle=\sum_i\langle\varphi,A_i^*A_i\psi\rangle=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle,$$

that is, $V^*V=1$. At the same time, the theory requires the $A_i$ not to commute: in general $A_iA_j\neq A_jA_i$. This requirement is merely stated in the present chapter; its weight only becomes visible in Chapter 6—non-commutativity will be the source of the mechanism of entropy growth.

### 4.3 Properties of the Isometry

Three properties follow immediately from $V^*V=1$. First, norm-preservation: $\|V\psi\|=\|\psi\|$; the embedding does not change the length of a state. Second, injectivity: if $V\psi=0$, then $\|\psi\|=\|V\psi\|=0$, hence $\psi=0$; distinct states remain distinct after embedding. Third, no loss of information: acting on $V\psi$, the adjoint $V^*$ recovers $\psi$ exactly, since $V^*V\psi=\psi$—$V^*$ is a left inverse of $V$. Together the three say: iteration erases no information, and the embedded state carries the entire content of the state before embedding.

But $V$ is not unitary. A unitary operator requires $V^*V=VV^*=1$ to hold simultaneously, whereas here $VV^*\neq1$. The picture is this: $V$ embeds $\mathcal{H}$ into $\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$, and its range is only a proper subspace of the target space; applying the embedding $V$ and then the adjoint $V^*$ yields the projection back onto that range, not the identity operator. In other words, there is a left inverse $V^*$ from $\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$ back to $\mathcal{H}$, but no right inverse by which $V$ fills the target space: from within the range one can return without loss, yet the whole space is not filled by the map.

Being isometric and being invertible are therefore not the same thing. The isometry guarantees that "information can be recovered without loss in the direction of embedding"; invertibility additionally demands that "every state has somewhere to come from." $V$ satisfies the former but not the latter, and this missing half is precisely the starting point of the unitarity question in the next chapter.

### 4.4 Global Property: Isometric Embedding with Conservation of Information

On the basis of the foregoing, the global property of the theory should be stated as "isometric embedding with conservation of information" rather than "strict unitarity." This is not a choice of wording but a choice between two different pictures. In the picture of unitary evolution, space is fixed, information is merely rearranged within it, and evolution is closed and reversible. In the picture of isometric evolution, information is likewise not lost (the left inverse $V^*$ guarantees recoverability), but the map does not fold back onto the full space—each step of iteration carries the state into a larger layer of space. In this picture, conservation of information and closure of space are two separate matters: this theory retains the former and does not require the latter. It should also be made explicit that "conservation of information" here is a structural statement (a left inverse exists; recovery without loss is possible), not a conserved quantity in any numerical sense.

The weight of this formulation is discharged in the next chapter. The iteration chain grows layer by layer, and the unitarity question must be handled at the level of the direct limit; there, the divergence between "isometric embedding with conservation of information" and "strict unitarity" takes concrete form as the proposition that "unitarity can only be recovered on a larger space." In terminology, operators of the kind of $V$ are recorded in this book as isometric (not unitary).

### 4.5 Non-Commutativity as the Source of Dynamics (Preview)

Finally, we point to the interface between this chapter and Chapter 6. The isometry of $V$ guarantees conservation, not change; if iteration is to serve as dynamics, the seed of change must grow elsewhere. It grows in the non-commutativity of the $A_i$: when $V$ contains non-commuting $A_i$, the very first step of iteration entangles the new node with the earlier part, and entropy begins to increase along the direction of iteration. The isometry is responsible for "not losing," and the non-commutativity for "changing"; only together do they constitute the complete picture of the dynamics.

Chapter 6 will also examine a contrasting case: the special case in which the $A_i$ degenerate to scalars. There, the first step of iteration produces no entanglement, and after folding back into $\mathcal{H}$ the origin state remains unmoved. Setting the general case beside this special case makes visible where the theory's "motion" comes from—not from the isometry, but from the non-commutation of the $A_i$.

**Key points of this chapter**

- The iteration rule $V$ is an independent third element: it cannot be derived from Elements One and Two, and the theory's flexibility is concentrated here.
- The form of $V$ is $V\psi=\sum_i e_i\otimes A_i\psi$, with the $A_i$ non-commuting and $\sum_iA_i^*A_i=1$, so that $V^*V=1$.
- The isometry yields: $V$ is injective, norm-preserving, and loses no information ($V^*$ is a left inverse); but $VV^*\neq1$, so $V$ is not unitary.
- The global property is stated as "isometric embedding with conservation of information" rather than "strict unitarity."
- Non-commutativity is the source of dynamics; its consequences for entropy growth, and the contrast with the scalar special case, are developed in Chapter 6.

## Chapter 5 Iteration Chains, the Direct Limit, and the Unitarity Question

Applied once, the iteration rule $V$ produces a state on a new layer of space; applied more than once, it produces a chain of states growing layer by layer. This chapter gives the mathematical form of the chain, houses it in the direct limit, and then addresses head-on the question left open by Chapter 4: isometric iteration is not unitary evolution—where did the unitarity go?

### 5.1 The Iteration Chain

Starting from $\psi_0\in\mathcal{H}$ and applying $V$ successively, one obtains a sequence of states, each dwelling on one layer of space:

$$\psi_0\in\mathcal{H},\quad \psi_1=V\psi_0\in\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H},\quad \psi_2\in(\mathbb{C}^n)^{\otimes2}\otimes\mathcal{H},\ \dots$$

Each step adds one node at the position of the first tensor factor. After the $k$-th step, the state lives in the tensor product of $k$ factors of $\mathbb{C}^n$ and one factor of $\mathcal{H}$: the number of nodes increases monotonically along the chain, and the terminal factor is always $\mathcal{H}$. That this extension can proceed without end is precisely due to the self-referential structure of Element One: the terminal factor $\mathcal{H}$ can always be expanded once more as $\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$, making room for the next step of iteration. By Chapter 4, every step is injective and norm-preserving, so no step along the chain loses information, and the state on the $k$-th layer carries the entire history of the preceding $k$ steps.

One should note what is special about this picture: the chain is not "one and the same state moving through a fixed family of spaces," but "one state unfolding through spaces that extend layer by layer." Space itself grows with the iteration; this is not an added postulate but the layer-by-layer realization of the self-referential structure $\mathcal{H}\cong\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$ along the direction of iteration. As for the shape of the chain—how the nodes are connected, how far correlations extend—that depends on the concrete form of $V$ and belongs to the numerical discussion of Chapter 8.

### 5.2 A State in the Direct Limit

The layer spaces $(\mathbb{C}^n)^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathcal{H}$ are linked layer by layer by the isometric embeddings, and the direct limit fuses these layers into one total space. Plainly put, this construction places all the layers into a single total space while preserving the linking relations between adjacent layers, so that the state on any layer finds its place within it. Only with this total space does "the entire chain" acquire a single mathematical identity: the entire chain is a state in the direct limit. The state at any finite stage of the chain is not an independent object but the manifestation of this state on a finite layer; apart from the direct limit, there is nowhere to house "the entire chain."

This arrangement will reappear later in the chapter: the direct limit is not only the space in which the entire chain is housed, but also the larger space to which one must turn when discussing the recovery of unitarity. The next section first folds the interlayer language back into the interior of $\mathcal{H}$.

### 5.3 Folding Back into $\mathcal{H}$: $W=\sum_iS_iA_i$

After $V$ acts, the state leaves $\mathcal{H}$ and enters $\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$. But with the help of the unitary isomorphism $U$ of Element One, the process can be viewed folded back into the interior of $\mathcal{H}$. For any $\psi$:

$$U^*V\psi=U^*\sum_i e_i\otimes A_i\psi=\sum_iS_iA_i\psi.$$

This naturally defines a self-map of $\mathcal{H}$,

$$W=\sum_iS_iA_i,$$

satisfying $W\psi=U^*V\psi$: to embed first and then fold back is the same as applying $W$ directly on $\mathcal{H}$.

$W$ inherits the isometric property. Using the range-orthogonality $S_i^*S_j=\delta_{ij}$ established in Chapter 2, a direct computation gives

$$W^*W=\sum_{i,j}A_i^*S_i^*S_jA_j=\sum_iA_i^*A_i=1.$$

So $W$ is an isometric operator on $\mathcal{H}$. And since $V$ is not surjective (were it surjective, one would have $VV^*=1$, contradicting Element Three), $W$ is not surjective either: it is the kind of isometry described in Chapter 4—isometric (not unitary)—also called a one-sided isometry. At this point the same iteration process has two equivalent writings: $V$ is the isometric embedding between layers, $W$ is the one-sided isometry inside $\mathcal{H}$, and the two are transcribed into each other via $U$. Folding back is not a superfluous trick: $W$ lets the entire iteration process be discussed uniformly within $\mathcal{H}$, and both the unitarity discussion of the next section and the examination of the origin state in Chapter 6 ($W\psi_0=\psi_0$) rely on it.

### 5.4 The Wold Decomposition and the Minimal Unitary Dilation

We can now answer head-on the question left by Chapter 4: $V$ (or $W$, after folding back) is not unitary—where did the unitarity go? To the author's knowledge, $W$, as a one-sided isometry, has a minimal unitary dilation on the direct limit (Wold decomposition), so unitarity can only be recovered on a larger space. This point needs to be checked item by item against the literature.

The spatial levels of this statement deserve to be made explicit. Within $\mathcal{H}$, the evolution is carried by the one-sided isometry $W$: it has a left inverse and loses no information, but it is not a unitary operator; as long as one does not leave $\mathcal{H}$, this property does not change under a change of writing. To seek unitarity, one must step into a larger space containing $\mathcal{H}$—and by the judgment above, the direct limit assumes precisely this role. "Wold decomposition" and "minimal unitary dilation" appear here only as terms, referring to the fact itself of "recovering unitarity on a larger space"; this book makes no stronger assertion on that basis, and the relevant operator-theoretic details remain to be checked item by item against the literature. Retaining this qualifier verbatim in the text is meant to keep an operator-theoretic claim that has not yet been verified item by item from bearing more expressive weight than its evidence warrants; the entire discussion of unitarity in this section should be read at this strength.

### 5.5 Physical Meaning: The Isometric-Conservation View and the Unitary View

The relation between the two views can be summarized as follows. The unitary view regards evolution as reversible rearrangement within a fixed space: conservation of information and closure of space are bundled together, and evolution produces no new "position." The isometric-conservation view takes these two matters apart: conservation of information requires only that a left inverse exist ($V^*V=1$), not that the space be closed ($VV^*\neq1$); the space extends layer by layer, and information is carried forward with it. This theory takes the isometric-conservation view as its basic formulation and regards unitarity as a derived property at a larger level of space, not as a fundamental requirement. Accordingly, the word "evolution" in this book does not imply that space remains fixed; the reader should not confuse it with unitary evolution within a fixed space.

This choice does not alter the physical content of "no loss of information": under either formulation, every step is injective and norm-preserving, and the historical information is not lost in the iteration. The disagreement between the two views is not over whether information is conserved, but over the manner in which the conservation is realized—rearrangement inside a closed box, or being carried through layer-by-layer growth. The mathematical structure is now complete; the next chapter discusses entropy on this basis.

**Key points of this chapter**

- The iteration chain $\psi_0\in\mathcal{H}$, $\psi_1=V\psi_0\in\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$, $\psi_2\in(\mathbb{C}^n)^{\otimes2}\otimes\mathcal{H}$, …—each step adds one node at the position of the first tensor factor.
- The entire chain is a state in the direct limit.
- With the help of $U$, the iteration can be folded back into $\mathcal{H}$: $W=\sum_iS_iA_i$, and from $S_i^*S_j=\delta_{ij}$ one obtains $W^*W=1$; $W$ is a one-sided isometry.
- To the author's knowledge, $W$, as a one-sided isometry, has a minimal unitary dilation on the direct limit (Wold decomposition), so unitarity can only be recovered on a larger space; this point needs to be checked item by item against the literature.
- The global picture: conservation of information does not require closure of space; the isometric-conservation view is the basic formulation, and unitarity is a derived property on a larger space.

## Chapter 6 The Low-Entropy Origin and the Mechanism of Entropy Growth

Taken together, the three elements immediately yield a non-trivial corollary: the origin state is low-entropy by nature. This chapter derives that corollary, explains the relativity of the zero entropy, gives the qualitative mechanism of entropy growth, and examines an important contrast—the scalar special case in which the origin state does not move at all.

### 6.1 The Low-Entropy Origin Is a Corollary

Applying the unitary isomorphism $U$ of Element One to $\psi_0$, and using the equivalent characterization $S_j^*\psi_0=c_j\psi_0$ of Element Two:

$$U\psi_0=\sum_ie_i\otimes S_i^*\psi_0=\sum_ie_i\otimes c_i\psi_0=c\otimes\psi_0,$$

where $c=\sum_ic_ie_i\in\mathbb{C}^n$; and from $\sum_j|c_j|^2=1$ one obtains $\|c\|=1$, so $c$ is a unit vector in $\mathbb{C}^n$. The entire derivation uses only Element One and Element Two and introduces no new assumption; the vector $c$ uses precisely the same set of coefficients as the fixed-point condition $\psi_0=\sum_ic_iS_i\psi_0$.

The conclusion: the low-entropy origin is a corollary, not a separate postulate. The theory needs no independent axiom to guarantee that "the origin is low-entropy"; as soon as the self-referential structure and the fixed-point condition hold, $U\psi_0=c\otimes\psi_0$ holds automatically. Under the decomposition $\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H}$, the origin state is strictly a product state, with entanglement entropy zero. Low entropy is not an initial condition placed at the head of the theory, but a result drawn from the structure; it needs no additional mechanism to arrange it.

### 6.2 The Product State and the Relativity of Zero Entropy

The state $c\otimes\psi_0$ is a product state: the $\mathbb{C}^n$ factor and the $\mathcal{H}$ factor each keep to themselves, with no correlation between the two sides, and the entanglement entropy computed for this bipartition is therefore zero. It should be noted that the zero entropy concerns the correlation between the two factors, not the entire content of the state: $\psi_0$ may still be richly structured within $\mathcal{H}$, and $c$ may be a superposition in $\mathbb{C}^n$ with every component nonzero. But this zero entropy holds only relative to the Cuntz decomposition. The "low entropy" in question is zero relative to the particular bipartition "$\mathbb{C}^n\,|\,\mathcal{H}$" given by the Cuntz algebra; it does not say that the origin state is structureless under any bipartition, any description. Read under a different decomposition, the same $\psi_0$ no longer automatically yields the verdict of zero entropy.

This relativity claim must be stated together with the corollary itself. If it is omitted, "low-entropy origin" will be misread as an absolute, decomposition-independent property—a claim the theory does not make. The accurate statement is: under the theory's own decomposition (the Cuntz decomposition), the entanglement entropy of the origin state is zero; this zero is relative.

### 6.3 Non-Commutativity Produces Entropy Growth

The first step of iteration gives $\psi_1=V\psi_0=\sum_ie_i\otimes A_i\psi_0$. When does this state remain a product state? Only when the $A_i\psi_0$ are collinear—that is, all multiples of one and the same vector—can $\psi_1$ be rewritten as "a factor in $\mathbb{C}^n$ ⊗ a factor in $\mathcal{H}$." If $V$ contains non-commuting $A_i$, the very first step of iteration entangles the new node with the earlier part, and entropy begins to increase along the direction of iteration. The intuitive mechanism: a non-commuting family of operators will in general not compress the image of $\psi_0$ onto a single ray; the branches $A_i\psi_0$ differ from one another, so the branch index on the first tensor factor (the new node) becomes correlated with the internal state on the $\mathcal{H}$ side, and the product structure breaks at the first step. The emergence of entanglement requires no external disturbance; it is the direct manifestation of non-commutativity in the first iteration.

Two points need emphasis. First, this is a qualitative mechanism: it explains why entropy begins to grow and along which direction; as for the quantitative behavior of the growth—rise followed by saturation, and how large the saturation value is—this chapter cites no numbers, and the saturation values are given in Chapter 8. Second, entropy growth is not an additional hypothesis, and it coexists without conflict with the "conservation of information" of Chapter 4: no loss of information says that nothing is erased along the entire chain; entropy growth says that the correlation between the new node and the earlier part is increasing. Conservation and growing entropy describe two faces of one and the same process. Read this together with the relativity claim of Section 6.2: the zero entropy of the origin state is a statement about the Cuntz bipartition; after the first iteration, the entropy read from that same bipartition is no longer zero.

### 6.4 The Scalar Special Case: Stasis

There is an important contrasting case: take $A_i=c_i$, letting the operator of each branch degenerate to a scalar. This choice satisfies the compatibility condition on $V$, since $\sum_iA_i^*A_i=\sum_i|c_i|^2=1$ (using $\sum_j|c_j|^2=1$ from Element Two); the $A_i$ then plainly commute with one another. Substituting into the iteration:

$$V\psi_0=\sum_ie_i\otimes c_i\psi_0=c\otimes\psi_0,$$

which is exactly $U\psi_0$—the new node does not entangle with the earlier part, and the first step of iteration produces no entropy at all. Folding back into $\mathcal{H}$ (with the $W=\sum_iS_iA_i$ of Section 5.3):

$$W\psi_0=\sum_iS_ic_i\psi_0=\sum_ic_iS_i\psi_0=\psi_0,$$

where the last step is precisely the fixed-point condition. The origin state remains unchanged: the iteration $W$ marks time in place on $\psi_0$, the entire chain does not unfold in this special case, and the mechanism of entropy growth never starts. This is the static special case.

Its role is to mark a degenerate corner of the space of operator choices. The gap between the general case (non-commuting $A_i$) and this special case is the entire source of "change" in the theory: how far the $A_i$ depart from scalars determines the speed and the bounds of entropy growth. Conversely, if the $A_i$ actually chosen lie close to this special case, the whole picture approaches stasis—the dynamical content of the theory hangs entirely on the choice of the $A_i$. This again confirms the claim of Chapter 4: the flexibility is concentrated in $V$.

### 6.5 The Meaning of the Arrow of Time

Read together with Chapter 5, this chapter shows three directions coinciding. The direction of entropy growth is the direction of iteration (Section 6.3); each step of iteration adds one node (Chapter 5); and the temporal order is defined by the iteration of $V$—this ontological statement is developed in Chapter 7. Within this framework, therefore, the direction of entropy growth = the direction of iteration = the direction of temporal order.

This correspondence is definitional: the theory defines temporal order by iteration and houses entropy growth in that same iteration, so the coincidence of the two directions is a matter of construction, not a law derived from something deeper. Nor does this framework claim thereby to explain any received arrow of time; such a claim would exceed what the framework can currently deliver. In this framework, the meaning of the "arrow of time" is precisely this: the entropy growth read along the direction of temporal order and the entropy growth read along the direction of iteration are one and the same thing. The question with real content is not "why the same direction," but how fast entropy grows and where it stops—this depends on the structure of the $A_i$ and the dimension of the bond space, and the saturation values are given in Chapter 8.

**Key points of this chapter**

- $U\psi_0=c\otimes\psi_0$ (with $c=\sum_ic_ie_i$ a unit vector): the low-entropy origin is a corollary, not a separate postulate.
- The origin state is a product state with entanglement entropy zero; this zero entropy holds only relative to the Cuntz decomposition.
- When $V$ contains non-commuting $A_i$, the first step of iteration entangles the new node with the earlier part, and entropy begins to increase along the direction of iteration; quantitative details (saturation values) are given in Chapter 8.
- The scalar special case $A_i=c_i$: $V\psi_0=c\otimes\psi_0$, and after folding back $W\psi_0=\psi_0$—the origin state does not move: the static special case.
- Direction of entropy growth = direction of iteration = direction of temporal order; this correspondence is definitional.

## Chapter 7 Temporal Order, Existence, and the Single-Chain Interpretation

The first six chapters completed the statement of the three elements and the construction of the iteration chain: the self-referential structure provides the self-similar decomposition of space, the origin state provides the starting point of iteration, and the iteration rule specifies how the chain is extended. This chapter discusses what this chain means for time and existence — what defines temporal order, to whom "existence" belongs, and what the single-chain interpretation chooses, what it declines, and which questions remain open.

### 7.1 Temporal order is defined by iteration: discrete time and discrete space as one

Within this framework, temporal order is not an externally imposed background parameter; it is defined by the iteration of $V$: each step of iteration adds one node, and the order of the nodes is the order of before and after. What is called a "moment" is the position of a node on the chain; what is called "before" and "after" is the creation separation. Time is not a stage on which iteration takes place; it is the counting of the iterative process itself. To look for another kind of time apart from iteration has no object within this framework.

$$\psi_0\in\mathcal{H},\quad \psi_1\in\mathbb{C}^n\otimes\mathcal{H},\quad \psi_2\in(\mathbb{C}^n)^{\otimes2}\otimes\mathcal{H},\ \dots$$

Each step adds a node at the first tensor-factor position; in general, $\psi_k\in(\mathbb{C}^n)^{\otimes k}\otimes\mathcal{H}$. At the same time, the addition of nodes one by one also generates spatial structure: the connections between nodes are given by the iteration rule, and the lengthening of the chain is the growth of space. Discrete time and discrete space are therefore one — they are not two independent settings but two aspects of a single generative process. There is no layered arrangement in which space is built first and time is then made to flow through it; there is only a chain of nodes that keeps growing longer.

### 7.2 No "void in between" between adjacent stages

Between two adjacent stages there is no "void in between". The passage from $\psi_k$ to $\psi_{k+1}$ is not a traversal of an empty interval, nor a wait for some external clock to complete one tick; iteration is the entire way in which things happen, and adjacent stages join directly. To ask "what happens between two steps" has no referent in this framework: there is no process finer than "one step", and there is no gap that needs filling.

The classical picture imagines time as a uniformly flowing container in which events do or do not occur; this framework abolishes the container itself. The unit of happening is one step of iteration, and the scale of time is the node order. This abolition is not a restriction but a clarification: any question that can be posed only with the help of a "void in between" is not a question within this framework.

### 7.3 The entire chain exists tenselessly

"Existence" belongs to the entire chain, and "evolution" is only a relation between adjacent stages. The entire chain is a state in the direct limit; it exists tenselessly: it is not "now being" generated, nor was it "once" generated. Generation is not an external event happening to the chain; it is a structural relation between adjacent stages inside the chain. When the entire chain is compared to the complete history of the universe, one should note: this history is not a series of events swept along by a flow of time, but a whole that is complete with respect to its mode of existence.

Before and after are the chain's intrinsic order, not something flowing through it from outside. To ask "which step has the entire chain now reached" is meaningless in this picture: "now" is not a property of the entire chain. At most it is perspectival language concerning some stage within the chain — and "why this one stage is taken as the present" is precisely the open question to be addressed at the end of this chapter.

### 7.4 Two contradictions: their assignment to levels and their dissolution

The contradiction between "existence and non-existence" arises as follows. From the perspective inside a stage, a past stage is no longer in the present, and so it "does not exist"; from the perspective of the entire chain, every stage is on the chain, and nothing vanishes. The contradiction comes from mixing statements that belong to two different levels. Once the two levels are kept apart, the contradiction dissolves: at the level of the chain, existence is tenseless and integral; only between stage and stage can relational language such as "precedes" and "follows" apply. Both statements hold, each on its own level, so long as neither crosses levels.

The contradiction between "evolving and not evolving" dissolves in the same way. The entire chain exists tenselessly, so it makes no sense to say that "the whole is evolving"; yet "evolution", as a relation between adjacent stages, holds everywhere within the chain. Thus "the universe evolves" and "the universe exists tenselessly" do not conflict: the former describes relations within the chain, the latter describes the chain's mode of existence. Two propositions that appear to be in head-on opposition are in fact making two statements about two levels.

### 7.5 You of the previous moment are not erased

$V$ is isometric: $V^*V=1$; it is injective, norm-preserving, and loses no information. Hence all of the information of the previous stage is still in the current state; what has changed is its form of existence: it is no longer concentrated at one node but is dispersed in the correlations of the current state and in the entanglement with the environment. You of the previous moment are not erased; what changes is not "whether you are" but "in what form you are".

The intuition of "erasure" comes from the classical overwriting picture: the new state is written down and the old state wiped away. In the picture of states there is no such overwriting. The entropy-growth mechanism of Chapter 6 is the other side of the same fact: the non-commuting $A_i$ entangle the new node with the old part, and entropy increases along the direction of iteration — which shows precisely that the old information has not been deleted but has entered a larger correlational structure. The past is present in the form of correlation.

### 7.6 Definition and interpretive choice: two claims of different kinds

Two sentences must be stated apart. The first: "only self-iteration gives rise to temporal order" is a definition. Within this framework, the term "temporal order" refers precisely to the node order given by the iteration of $V$; this is not a proposition awaiting proof but a stipulation. It may be criticized as too narrow or too broad, but it is not a falsifiable claim.

The second: "there is only one chain" is an interpretive choice, not a theorem. The mathematical objects — the states and chains in the direct limit — do not by themselves force a single interpretation; to read the entire chain as the one chain, rather than as one among many chains, is an interpretive decision about one and the same mathematical structure. This decision is neither compelled by derivation nor casually replaceable; its costs and its boundaries are the subject of the next two sections.

### 7.7 The treatment of decoherence branches

The branches produced by decoherence still exist mathematically. This framework interprets them as the internal incoherent structure of this chain at a given stage: within a stage, the correlations among some subsystems take an incoherent form, and these incoherent parts are what are usually called "branches".

The key to this treatment is that the incoherent parts within a stage have no temporal order. The mixedness of subsystems and spacelike correlations are "transverse" structures — correlations among different degrees of freedom within one and the same stage; there is no before and after among them, and they do not constitute another timeline. Accordingly, the branches that still exist mathematically acquire no ontological status: they are not other histories on other chains but the internal shape of one stage of this chain. Correspondingly, the claim of v2 and earlier versions that treated decoherence branches as independently existing histories has been retracted (Chapter 10).

### 7.8 What is not promised and what remains open

The boundaries of the single-chain interpretation must be stated as they are. This framework is not committed to a many-worlds ontology, and it has not solved the self-locating problem. "Why this one stage is taken as the present" remains open: since existence belongs to the entire chain, the framework currently has no answer as to where the sense of "now" comes from. This gap is not papered over; it is recorded in the list of open questions (Chapter 10).

At the same time, the cost of the single-chain interpretation must be reckoned clearly. The consequence of the fourth retraction is that this framework cannot borrow the explanatory power of many worlds. Choosing one chain means giving up a ready-made resource of probabilities and perspectives in exchange for clarity of internal structure. Whether this exchange is worthwhile depends on whether the open questions can be advanced; in this chapter, our task is only to draw the boundary clearly.

**Key points of this chapter**

- Temporal order is defined by the iteration of $V$, with one node added at each step; discrete time and discrete space are one — two aspects of a single generative process.
- Between adjacent stages there is no "void in between"; "existence" belongs to the entire chain, the entire chain exists tenselessly, and "evolution" is only a relation between adjacent stages.
- The contradictions of "existence and non-existence" and of "evolving and not evolving" dissolve once they are assigned to two different levels.
- You of the previous moment are not erased: your information is dispersed in the correlations of the current state and in the entanglement with the environment.
- "Only self-iteration gives rise to temporal order" is a definition; "there is only one chain" is an interpretive choice, not a theorem; decoherence branches are the internal incoherent structure within a stage and do not constitute another timeline; this framework is not committed to a many-worlds ontology, and the self-locating problem remains open.

## Chapter 8 The Minimal Model: Emergence of a One-Dimensional Chain and Entropy Saturation

Of the three elements, the iteration rule $V$ has not yet been fixed by the first two — the theory's degrees of freedom are concentrated here. This chapter takes the most naive class of $V$ for a numerical experiment, as a negative (falsifying) test of the theory: it examines "what a casually chosen iteration rule gives", not "which physics the theory has already derived".

### 8.1 Model setup

The minimal model is set up as follows. The number of branches is $n=2$; the $A_i$ are generated at random, do not commute with one another, and satisfy $\sum_iA_i^*A_i=1$, so that $V^*V=1$ and $V$ is an isometric embedding; the starting point is a product state of finite bond dimension $D$.

It must be stated explicitly: the product state of finite bond dimension $D$ is a finite truncation of the infinite-dimensional case, and is not a genuine fixed point. The origin state $\psi_0$ of Chapter 3 lives in the infinite-dimensional $\mathcal{H}$ and is characterized by $S_j^*\psi_0=c_j\psi_0,\ \sum_j|c_j|^2=1$; the product state of finite bond dimension is only its finite-dimensional stand-in. The minimal model therefore simulates "iteration from an approximate starting point", not the full Element Two. This distinction is not a detail; it is the boundary of the model's status.

Why choose this class of $V$? Because $V$ has not yet been fixed by the first two elements, any additional design imposed on the $A_i$ would smuggle assumptions into the model; randomness, non-commutativity, and only the normalization constraint $\sum_iA_i^*A_i=1$ press the additional structure to a minimum. The model therefore measures not a carefully engineered rule but the generic behavior of this class of rules — and that is exactly why it can serve as a negative (falsifying) test.

### 8.2 Emergent geometry: a one-dimensional chain

Iteration adds nodes one by one at the first tensor-factor position, and the nodes line up in their order of creation. The numerical results show that the emergent geometry is a one-dimensional chain. For this class of random $V$, the correlations between nodes are distributed along the direction of the chain; space is neither a two-dimensional plane nor a three-dimensional network but a line.

This result is not assumed; it is a direct product of the iterative structure. Nothing in the setup imposes one-dimensionality: no lattice sites are presupposed, no adjacency relations are presupposed; the chain shape grows out of the iteration itself. The result also immediately yields a measurable object: along this chain, how correlations change with distance. That is the subject of the next section.

### 8.3 Mutual information decays exponentially with creation separation

The model measures the mutual information between nodes. The creation separation is the distance between two nodes in the node order. The result is: the mutual information decays exponentially with creation separation, with a decay rate of approximately 0.63 per step; the correlation length is approximately 1.6 steps. The two numbers are given side by side exactly as specified: the former characterizes the rate of decay, the latter the range over which correlations remain appreciable; we offer no further conversion or fitting account connecting the two.

The implication is direct: two nodes created several steps apart already have very small mutual information; correlations on the chain are short-ranged, and distant nodes share almost no information. Taken together, the decay rate and the correlation length give the correlational portrait of this chain. The manner of reporting is itself part of the qualification: we state only the two numbers given by the specification, introduce no decay formula, and do not treat the decay rate within the model as a measured value in nature. This short-rangedness echoes the entropy saturation of the next section.

### 8.4 Entropy first rises and then saturates

Along the direction of iteration, the entropy first rises and then saturates. The saturation value depends on the bond dimension $D$: for $D=2$ the saturation value is 0.50, for $D=4$ it is 1.12, and for $D=8$ it is 1.75, in units of nat, and always below $\ln D$.

The saturation value grows as $D$ increases, but for finite $D$ the entropy never exceeds its respective saturation value; the unit nat means that entropy is measured with the natural logarithm, and "always below $\ln D$" shows that the saturation value does not exhaust the ceiling allowed by the bond dimension $D$. This is consistent with the short-rangedness of the mutual information: the information carried by a new node is quickly accommodated by the finite capacity of the bond dimension, and the growth of entropy stops accordingly. This, of course, is only the finite-dimensional picture; the infinite-dimensional case is another matter, treated in the next section.

### 8.5 Finite-dimensional boundedness and the infinite-dimensional hypothesis

The finite-dimensional conclusion is: in finite dimensions, entropy growth is bounded; only when the bond space is infinite-dimensional can entropy grow in a sustained manner.

Caution is required here. The proposition "infinite-dimensional bond space ⟹ entropy can grow in a sustained manner" is compatible with the theory's infinite-dimensional ontology — the ontological commitment of Chapter 1 is precisely states in an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space — but it must be stated as an assumption and must not be written as a corollary: at present, no derivation from the three elements yields it directly. Note also the division of labor between the two conclusions: "in finite dimensions, entropy growth is bounded" is an observational result of the model; "only when the bond space is infinite-dimensional can entropy grow in a sustained manner" is an interpretation and extrapolation of that observation, and the two differ in evidential status. What the minimal model observes in finite dimensions is saturation only; the behavior in the infinite-dimensional case is an assumption, whose status is the same as the entries of the list of open questions — awaiting future derivation, future strengthening, or future abandonment. In other words, the saturation observed in this chapter is a property of bounded bond dimension, not an assertion by the theory about the entropy history of the universe; to conflate the two is to read an assumption as a corollary.

### 8.6 The nature of the negative (falsifying) test

The test performed by this model is a negative (falsifying) test: it rules out the claim that "this class of random $V$ directly yields three-dimensional geometry". If someone were to claim that casually chosen non-commuting $A_i$ can produce three-dimensional space, the model's result refutes that claim — what is obtained is a one-dimensional chain, with short-ranged correlations and entropy saturation.

But it must equally be stated explicitly: it does not rule out a $V$ with additional structure. If special design is built into the $A_i$, the situation can differ; in that case, however, the three-dimensionality would come from the additional structure, not from the generic properties of the iteration rule. How space comes to be three-dimensional is therefore not derived. This gap has been entered in the list of open questions (Chapter 10). It is also consistent with the first retraction: the association of $n = 2^d$ with spatial dimension has been retracted, and the minimal model's result — random $A_i$ with $n=2$ giving a one-dimensional chain — accords with that retraction.

### 8.7 Reproducibility

Regarding the model's standing as a test, only one necessary sentence is required: the model involves only low-dimensional matrix operations and is fully reproducible. All the numbers reported above come from this directly repeatable computation; beyond this, this section describes no implementation details and supplies no code, seeds, or parameter provenance for the model.

Reproducibility matters because the model's entire function is falsification: a computation that cannot be repeated rules out nothing. Precisely because the computation involves only low-dimensional matrix operations, every number in this chapter can in principle be re-run and checked by anyone; the model's claims therefore stop within the range they can defend.

**Key points of this chapter**

- Model setup: $n=2$; the $A_i$ are random, non-commuting, and satisfy $\sum_iA_i^*A_i=1$; the starting point is a product state of finite bond dimension $D$ — a finite truncation of the infinite-dimensional case, not a genuine fixed point.

- The model rules out "this class of random $V$ directly yielding three-dimensional geometry" and does not rule out a $V$ with additional structure; how space comes to be three-dimensional is not derived; the model involves only low-dimensional matrix operations and is fully reproducible.

## Chapter 9 The Conditional Particle Module: Weyl Points and Chern Numbers

This chapter examines how particles could arise within this framework. It must be declared at the outset: this is a conditional module, not a core corollary; its argument rests on two premises, and if the premises are not met, the argument fails. The chapter adds nothing to the core claims; it only shows which structures the theory can connect to under additional conditions.

### 9.1 The status of the module

Every argument in this chapter hangs on two premises. This is not modest phrasing but the fact of the argumentative structure: if the premises do not hold, the chain of derivation does not start. The conclusions of this chapter therefore cannot be turned around and used as support for the theory's core claims; the core claims — the three elements, the iteration chain, the low-entropy origin, the entropy-growth mechanism — do not depend on anything in this chapter.

To call the module "conditional" means that each of its conclusions bears the imprint of the premises. The value of a conditional module lies in exploration: if the theory is to grow particles, what conditions are needed, and how far can one get? The reader should read this chapter as a side line: the main line lies in Chapters 7 and 8, the side line is here, and every step of the side line may be voided should the premises fail.

### 9.2 The two premises

Premise 1: a translation lattice exists. Premise 2: particles are isolated degeneracy points of a family of complex Hermitian Hamiltonians over the momentum space $T^d$.

On a translation lattice, momentum space is the $d$-dimensional torus $T^d$; the Hamiltonian varies with momentum and forms a family of complex Hermitian matrices; "particle", on this argumentative route, is defined as an isolated degeneracy point of this family of matrices. Together, the two premises fix the mathematical language of this chapter: only because there is a translation lattice can one speak of momentum space; only because there is a family of Hamiltonians can one speak of degeneracy and energy gap. Consequently, once the premises fail, all the arguments below fail with them.

### 9.3 The codimension argument

The codimension of degeneracy of a complex Hermitian family is 3. The standard codimension count runs as follows: near a degeneracy point, the ways in which a Hermitian matrix departs from degeneracy are controlled by three independent real parameters, and degeneracy requires all three parameters to vanish simultaneously. Hence, in a $d$-dimensional parameter space, the degeneracy set generically has dimension $d-3$.

Cases can then be distinguished. For $d=3$, the degeneracy set is zero-dimensional: degeneracies are isolated points. For $d=2$, the degeneracy set is one-dimensional: degeneracies form lines. For $d=1$, the dimension is negative: generically no degeneracy exists, that is, there is no isolated degeneracy. Hence only for $d=3$ do objects of the kind "isolated degeneracy point" exist generically. This step is pure parameter counting and depends on no detail of the dynamics — which is at once where its reliability and its limits lie: it says what $d=3$ can accommodate, not why the world takes $d=3$.

### 9.4 Topological charge: integer Chern numbers

An isolated degeneracy point carries an integer Chern-number charge, corresponding to $\pi_2(\mathbb{C}P^\infty)=\mathbb{Z}$ (numerically verified). Intuitively: enclose a degeneracy point; the eigen-directions over a two-dimensional sphere in parameter space form a map, and the homotopy class of this map is labeled by an integer; that integer is the Chern-number charge.

Integer charge brings stability: a small perturbation can only move a charged degeneracy point, not annihilate it; to annihilate it, it must meet a degeneracy point carrying charge of the opposite sign. This is precisely where the "stable" in "stable point-like charged particles" comes from. Note that this argument remains within the premises: given a family of Hamiltonians, then degeneracy points; given isolated degeneracy points, then charge.

### 9.5 Low-energy structure: the Weyl form $\pm\sigma\cdot k$

Near an isolated degeneracy point, the low-energy structure takes the Weyl form $\pm\sigma\cdot k$. Two immediate consequences follow. First, the two-valuedness of $\sigma$ corresponds to spin 1/2. Second, the two signs give degeneracy points in pairs — particle–antiparticle pairing.

That is to say, when the premises hold, particles on this route naturally carry the structures of spin 1/2 and particle–antiparticle pairing. This is the part of the module closest to known physical structures; but its conditionality must be kept in view throughout — structural resemblance is not derivation.

### 9.6 Mass and quantization

The opening of an energy gap gives mass: when the degeneracy is lifted, the zero gap becomes a finite gap, and this gap is the source of mass within this module. The single-valuedness of phase in the self-cyclic structure makes the particle level quantized: around one closed loop, the phase must return to itself; single-valuedness forbids arbitrary continuous values, and the allowed values are therefore discrete.

Taken together, these two give the module's picture of particles: there is charge, there is spin, there is a pairing structure, there is a source of mass, and there are discrete values. Within its premises this picture is self-contained; beyond the premises, it promises nothing.

### 9.7 The twofold gap

Gap one: selecting $d=3$ requires an additional selection principle — "the theory must contain stable point-like charged particles". This principle takes "such particles exist" as its starting point, and that is precisely the conclusion we would like the theory to account for; using it to select the dimension carries the risk of circularity. The codimension count shows that $d=3$ can accommodate isolated degeneracy points; it does not show why the world takes $d=3$.

Gap two: the irregular causal network grown by $V$ has no translation lattice, and this argument loses its footing. Premise 1 has no grounding in the framework's own structure: what the minimal model gives is a one-dimensional chain, still further from a translation lattice. Where the family of Hamiltonians required by Premise 2 would come from is likewise unexplained. With the two gaps superposed, the conditional character of the module is unmistakable.

### 9.8 Conclusion: not a core corollary

This module is not a core corollary. Its value lies in exhibiting the conditions of connection: if a translation lattice exists, and if particles can be defined as isolated degeneracy points of a family of complex Hermitian Hamiltonians over momentum space, then this framework can link up with structures such as Chern-number charge, spin 1/2, particle–antiparticle pairing, mass, and quantization. But these "ifs" are themselves not guaranteed by the theory.

Whether the module stands or falls depends on the first entries of the list of open questions (Chapter 10). In this sense, the title of this chapter already contains its conclusion: Weyl points and Chern numbers belong to a conditional module, not to the core.

**Key points of this chapter**

- This chapter is a conditional module, not a core corollary; if the two premises are not met, the argument fails.
- The two premises: a translation lattice exists; particles are isolated degeneracy points of a family of complex Hermitian Hamiltonians over the momentum space $T^d$.
- The codimension of degeneracy of a complex Hermitian family is 3: for $d=3$ degeneracies are isolated points, for $d=2$ they form lines, and for $d=1$ there is no isolated degeneracy.
- Isolated degeneracy points carry an integer Chern-number charge, corresponding to $\pi_2(\mathbb{C}P^\infty)=\mathbb{Z}$ (numerically verified); the low-energy structure takes the Weyl form $\pm\sigma\cdot k$, with spin 1/2 and particle–antiparticle pairing; the opening of an energy gap gives mass; the single-valuedness of phase in the self-cyclic structure makes the particle level quantized.
- The twofold gap: selecting $d=3$ requires an additional selection principle, which carries the risk of circularity; the irregular causal network has no translation lattice, and the argument loses its footing.

## Chapter 10 Open Questions and the Retraction List

This chapter sets out the theory's two lists: the problems not yet solved, and the claims already retracted. Both lists are given item by item, with nothing added and nothing removed; they are not appendix-like jottings but constituent parts of the theory's self-positioning. How a programme treats what it has not solved and what it has given up says more about its character than what it claims to have achieved.

### 10.1 Open questions

The following ten items are problems the theory explicitly has not solved at present. Each item carries one or two sentences of comment; the comments only describe where the gap lies and do not announce solutions. This list is also the raw material for the route forward in Chapter 11: the ordering of priorities is derived entirely from it, and no new aims are introduced.

1. **The tension between $d=3$ and the translation lattice.** The particle module requires a translation lattice and a three-dimensional momentum space, while the iteration of $V$ grows an irregular causal network; the twofold gap of Chapter 9 arises from this, and it remains unsolved.
2. **What additional structure the $A_i$ need in order to produce three-dimensional connectivity.** The minimal model shows that random $A_i$ give a one-dimensional chain; what additional structure the $A_i$ would need in order to yield a three-dimensional connectivity structure has no answer at present.
3. **The origin of the discrete step size.** Temporal order is defined by iteration, with one node added at each step; why the step size is discrete, and whether there is a deeper origin, is unexplained.
4. **How the growing network maintains Lorentz invariance.** The network given by iteration is in a state of growth; how such a network could give rise to and maintain Lorentz invariance has not yet been addressed.
5. **How the metric is read out from mutual information.** Mutual information characterizes the strength of correlation between nodes; a quantitative correspondence from mutual information to the metric has not yet been established, and the minimal model gives only a decay rate and a correlation length.
6. **The preferred basis.** Decoherence branches are interpreted in this framework as the internal incoherent structure within a stage, but in which basis the branches are delineated — the preferred basis problem — remains unsolved.
7. **The Born rule.** Whence the weights of probability come, the three elements provide no derivation; the Born rule remains to be dealt with.
8. **The values of the coefficients $c_i$ and the operators $A_i$.** What values the coefficients of the origin state and the operators of the iteration rule take is not given by the theory; this vacancy is directly related to the status of $V$ as an independent element — its plasticity is precisely where the theory's degrees of freedom lie.
9. **The concrete values of masses and spins.** The particle module gives structural conclusions (the Weyl form, Chern-number charge, pairing); it gives no concrete mass or spin values for any particle.
10. **Personal identity and experience — this framework gives no conclusion.** Chapter 7 explains that the information of past stages is dispersed in entanglement, but this is not an answer to personal identity and experience; the question remains open, and this framework gives no conclusion.

The ten items are not evenly distributed: the first five concern geometry and spacetime; the sixth and seventh concern probability and interpretation; the eighth and ninth concern parameters and values; the tenth concerns experience itself. This distribution roughly maps the theory's current working front: the earlier a question stands, the closer it is to structure; the later it stands, the closer to details and boundaries.

### 10.2 The retraction list

The following four retractions target claims of v2 and earlier versions (the 11-axiom system and the physical-constant fitting route). Each item gives the reason for retraction. A retraction is an explicit abandonment, with no reserved clauses: the four claims no longer appear in v3.0, neither plainly nor in weakened form.

1. **The association of $n = 2^d$ with spatial dimension — retracted.** Reason: this association was never derived; the minimal model shows precisely that random $A_i$ (with $n=2$) give a one-dimensional chain. There is no established correspondence between the number of branches and spatial dimension, and to posit one as an axiom is an overclaim.
2. **Identifying the origin postulate with the wavelet scaling equation — retracted.** Reason: for the sampling vector of the wavelet scaling equation, the sum of squared coefficients is 1/2 rather than 1, so it is not a normalized fixed point; the origin state requires $\sum_j|c_j|^2=1$. The two are alike in form but different in substance, and the identification fails.
3. **The setting of a continuous clock paired with discrete space — retracted.** Reason: temporal order is defined by the iteration of $V$, with one node added at each step, and discrete time and discrete space are one (Chapter 7); in this picture, pairing it with a continuous clock is both unnecessary and inconsistent with the discrete temporal order defined by iteration.
4. **Treating decoherence branches as independently existing histories — retracted.** Reason: the single-chain interpretation interprets decoherence branches as the internal incoherent structure of one stage of this chain (Chapter 7); to treat them as independently existing histories is to commit to a many-worlds ontology, which exceeds what the framework can guarantee. Consequence: this framework cannot borrow the explanatory power of many worlds.

The four retractions concern, respectively, spatial dimension, the origin postulate, the setting of time, and the interpretive stance, covering claims at four levels of the earlier versions. Retraction is not the quiet deletion of conclusions; it is the placing on record of "why they were given up". The value of such a record is the subject of the next section.

### 10.3 The methodological significance of retraction

Retraction is the theory's record of self-correction and a sign of health in a research programme. What the four retractions have in common is that each retracted claim "said more than could be derived". Retraction contracts the range of the theory's claims from "what has been said" to "what can be defended"; a programme that never retracts anything cannot distinguish persistence from rigidity.

Taken together, the retraction list and the list of open questions constitute the self-positioning of v3.0: what has not yet been achieved (but may be), and what has been given up (with reasons stated). Unlike open questions, retractions need not wait for future resolution; they are already complete. Their function is to mark out roads that should not be taken again, and to bind later authors: to raise any of these four claims again, one must first overturn the reasons for retraction recorded here. This positioning is updated as derivation and testing progress; the lists themselves are part of the theory — and its most honest part.

- There are ten open questions, listed item by item with nothing added and nothing removed; the tenth is "personal identity and experience — this framework gives no conclusion".
- There are four retractions: the association of $n = 2^d$ with spatial dimension; identifying the origin postulate with the wavelet scaling equation; the setting of a continuous clock paired with discrete space; treating decoherence branches as independently existing histories.
- The reason for the wavelet-scaling retraction: the sum of squared coefficients for the sampling vector is 1/2 rather than 1, so it is not a normalized fixed point.
- The consequence of the fourth retraction: this framework cannot borrow the explanatory power of many worlds.
- Retraction is the theory's record of self-correction and a sign of health in a research programme.

## Chapter 11 Conclusion: The Self-Positioning of the Research Programme

This chapter closes the book: taking stock of what has and has not been achieved, fixing the standards of statement, and setting out the route forward. There is only one legitimate way to write the conclusion — the way that matches the theory's actual standing; everything in this chapter is an elaboration of that sentence.

### 11.1 What has been achieved

Four achievements can be reported as they are. First, internal basic consistency: among the three elements, and between the elements and the corollaries, there is no mutual conflict, and the derivation chain from the iteration rule to the iteration chain, the direct limit, the low-entropy origin, and the entropy-growth mechanism runs through unbroken. Second, clear structure: what is a definition, what is a corollary, what is an assumption, what is an interpretive choice, and what is a conditional module are explicitly labeled throughout the book — the low-entropy origin is a corollary, infinite-dimensional entropy growth is an assumption, the single chain is an interpretive choice, and the particle module is a conditional module.

Third, a reproducible minimal model: the model of Chapter 8 involves only low-dimensional matrix operations and is fully reproducible, and its numbers (a decay rate of approximately 0.63 per step, a correlation length of approximately 1.6 steps, saturation values of 0.50, 1.12, and 1.75) come from a directly repeatable computation. Fourth, the three-element architecture itself: Element One (the self-referential structure), Element Two (the origin state), and Element Three (the iteration rule) are independent of one another, can be stated separately, and can be replaced or examined separately — each block of the theory can be criticized on its own, without implicating the rest. This architecture also gives the theory's weaknesses a definite address: the plasticity is concentrated in Element Three.

### 11.2 What has not been achieved

The most important unachieved item is a single one: no experimentally testable prediction has yet been produced. The theory at present has no number that can be compared with measurement. The numbers reported by the minimal model characterize the model's own properties — they rule out "this class of random $V$ directly yielding three-dimensional geometry", but they are not observables concerning the external world. The ten open questions of Chapter 10 are precisely the list of the distance between the theory and the empirical interface.

The size of this gap should be acknowledged as it is. Before testable predictions appear, the theory's appeal can come only from its internal structure and its interpretive picture, not from comparison with the world. This is one of the reasons why v3.0 comprehensively revises v2 and earlier versions (the 11-axiom system and the physical-constant fitting route): the earlier versions took "structural resemblance" for "empirical success", and v3.0 no longer does so.

### 11.3 Standards of statement

The standards of statement are determined by the actual standing. The theory is at present an internally basically consistent, clearly structured research programme with a reproducible minimal model; but it has not yet produced any experimentally testable prediction. No statement about it may exceed this range: one must not claim that it is "correct", "verified", or "consistent with experiment"; one must not revive inflated claims of the kind "physical constants have been derived"; and one must not present the conditional module or the interpretive choices as proven conclusions.

At the same time, the statement should not fall below the actual either: the theory has at least given a clear three-element architecture, a derivation chain that runs through, a reproducible minimal model, and two honest lists. The claim that it is "devoid of content" is equally at odds with the facts. The appropriate statement lies between the two, and its canonical form is the positioning statement at the end of the text.

### 11.4 The route forward

The route forward derives its priorities from the open questions and adds no new commitments. Ordered by structural position: first priority is $d=3$ and the translation lattice (open questions 1 and 2), which determine whether the particle module can move from conditional status to internal corollary; second is the correspondence between geometry and information (open questions 4 and 5) — Lorentz invariance and "how the metric is read out from mutual information" determine whether this framework can come into contact with the physics of spacetime; third is probability and interpretation (open questions 6 and 7); the origin of the step size, the values of the coefficients and operators, and the concrete values of masses and spins (open questions 3, 8, and 9) are difficult to approach before the structural questions are solved; personal identity and experience (open question 10) remains open, with no timetable and no place among the commitments.

This ordering is a working suggestion, not a new commitment. The theory gains no claims by virtue of the ordering; every step of progress must return to the two lists of Chapter 10 for examination, and new retractions and new open questions are equally legitimate forms of progress.

The book closes here. Dongshan Cosmic Information Theory v3.0 is an internally basically consistent, clearly structured research programme with a reproducible minimal model; but it has not yet produced any experimentally testable prediction. Before it yields a definite number or structure, it should not be presented as a correct physical theory.
